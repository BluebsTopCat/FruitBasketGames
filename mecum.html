Mini Mecum is the largest of the projects I’ve solo created, a 2022 adventure game about philosophy and absurdism. It released on Steam to positive reviews, and has since sold roughly six hundred copies.

I’ve since updated it more, to improve the graphical fidelity and patch a lot of bugs, and it's gotten to a relatively stable place. I made it in my free time as a high-schooler, and I reached far. I’m still phenomenally proud of what I did on it, but were I to redo it from scratch, I’d make a lot of changes. That's a good thing, though. It means I've gotten better at what I do.

Anyways, here's the best of the project!

Visuals

I started this project with the aim of an intentionally low-budget style. Every character is a stick figure, and most colours in the game are flat shades or simple gradients. Altogether, it gives the game a visual charm that seperates it from other low-budget Unity games.






Music and Ambience

The music I made for Mini Mecum is always eclectic, sometimes ambient, and has a few interesting properties. Namely, most tracks in the game consist of stacks of different songs that cycle and replace each other throughout an area.

The game uses a custom “transition zone” system in which custom objects are placed to designate and smoothly interpolate between different sound and music profiles. They trigger as a player moves through them, and define the ambience of the area.

Mechanics

There are a few mechanical implementations in the project that I’m still rather proud of– specifically, seamless portals, and gravity-defining walls.

Gravity-walls are pretty straightforward. Since I always want this game to have gravity, down is whatever direction the player is rotated– they’ll always fall below themselves, and so all walls have to do is rotate the player to face the normal of the mesh they’re walking on.

From there, I lerp the player’s rotation to the floors using quaternion math, and smooth it slightly to prevent sharp jitters.

Portal math is a bit more complicated, though.

I’m using Sebastion Lague’s portal method and style, but because of the physics-first character controller, I had to change the way that velocities are calculated and translated– making sure that momentum carried through.



The end result is flawed, with a single visible frame on translation between portals, but I think the effect is impressive enough anyways. In the future, I could fix this by putting portal math in a pre-render update loop, but that method doesn’t work well with unity’s URP.
